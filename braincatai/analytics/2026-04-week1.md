## Key Findings

### What's working
- **CTR is solid**: 6.39% average, peaks at 10–12% (Purr/Bones, Make Biscuits) — thumbnails grab attention
- **Algorithm pushes virality**: Cucumbers got 954 views with only 100 impressions → most views came from Shorts feed swipe-throughs (good algo signal)
- **Channel is finding traction**: 14 subs in first week from a brand-new channel is healthy

### What's broken
- **Retention is the real problem**: 7–20% across the board. **Hook fails in first 5–10 seconds.**
- **Top-CTR ≠ top-views**: "Purr Heal Bones" had 11.9% CTR but only 270 views — the title promised more than the video delivered, swipe-away in 4 sec
- **Sub conversion**: 3,092 views → 14 subs = **0.45%** (industry avg for Shorts is ~0.5–1%) — slightly below average

### Pattern: hooks are explanatory, not punchy
Current hooks (judging by titles/format) likely open with "Did you know…" or background context. Top-retention videos (#4, #7) are at 19–20% — still low. Target should be **40–50% retention** for algorithmic push.

## Recommendations (priority order)

1. **Fix the hook** — first 2 seconds must shock or contradict. Examples:
   - ❌ "Have you ever wondered why cats are afraid of cucumbers?"
   - ✅ "Your cat thinks a cucumber is a SNAKE. Here's why."

2. **Match payoff to title** — if title says "heal your bones," the answer better be in the video. The Purr/Bones flop suggests promise > delivery.

3. **Cut length to 35–45 sec** — current 49–59s is on the high side for low-retention Shorts. Shorter = higher % completion = better algo push.

4. **Double down on what worked**: Cucumbers got viral push, Make Biscuits got 10% CTR — these themes (cat behavior + psychology) are the niche. **Don't drift into generic facts.**

5. **Consistent thumbnail style** — 1% CTR (Cucumbers) vs 11.9% (Purr Bones) on same channel = thumbnail inconsistency. Lock template in style-guide.
