# RAIJIN — 雷神 | Japanese Zen Music for Inner Strength, Calm Power & Deep Focus

## Meta

- **Series:** Kanji-Concept — **KAMI (神) "Gods of Japan" sub-series** — seventh entry (after TSUKUYOMI, AMATERASU, HACHIMAN, BENZAITEN, KANNON, FUJIN).
- **Slug:** raijin-1h
- **Concept:** 雷神 (Raijin), the Shinto god of thunder and lightning — in the canonical pairing with FUJIN (most famously on Tawaraya Sōtatsu's folding screen and the Kaminarimon gate at Sensō-ji), Raijin is the elder brother. Where Fujin brings the moving wind, Raijin strikes with lightning and rolls low like distant thunder — but this video's angle is deliberately NOT rage or violence: his power is the kind that doesn't tremble. He stands as steady as the thunder itself, deep and even, never scattering into fury. This is the ideal anchor for **Inner Strength** — not overwhelming force, but an unshaken core. Sacred creature: the **raiju (雷獣)**, the thunder-beast of Japanese folklore, a wolf-or-weasel-like spirit said to ride lightning down to earth, here shown calm and grounded rather than leaping.

## §0 — Positioning & pre-production

- **🔒 KAMI series invariants (carried over):** each god manifests as a colossal figure formed of LIVING LIGHT/MIST — never a statue, never a carved idol, never a painted mural. A lone human figure anchors scale. Each god gets its own signature light colour — **RAIJIN = deep indigo-violet with flashes of electric white-blue light drifting within** — explicitly distinct from Tsukuyomi's pale silver-blue, Amaterasu's warm golden-amber, Hachiman's warm white-gold, Benzaiten's cool aquamarine-teal, Kannon's soft rose-pink/pearl-white, and Fujin's storm-grey/pale jade-green. Being Fujin's paired sibling deity, Raijin's palette should read as a deeper, more electric cousin of that storm family — not identical, immediately distinguishable side by side.
- **🔒 Break from the classical oni look — MANDATORY correction, same lesson as FUJIN.** Raijin is traditionally painted as a wild, fanged, horned, dark-skinned demon figure (the Sōtatsu screen counterpart to Fujin). Explicitly exclude: NO horns, NO fangs, NO clawed hands, NO monstrous or oni-like face, NO grimacing or threatening expression. Keep only what belongs to the series: a colossal figure of dense, volumetric, uneven-glowing light in the signature colour, calm-but-powerful — the whole point of this video is that his strength does NOT need to look violent to be immense.
- **Departure from the samurai convention (continues BENZAITEN/KANNON/FUJIN's precedent):** Raijin's human anchor is a **lone kagura ritualist** — a Shinto shrine musician performing a sacred rite, dressed in white-and-vermillion ceremonial robes (a kannushi's or kagura performer's dress), holding a hichiriki to his lips. He carries no weapon. This ties the human figure, the instrument, and the god's own theme together in one image, the same way FUJIN's komusō monk played the shakuhachi that leads that video's music.
- **Visual hook:** a lone Shinto kagura musician stands in a rain-slicked shrine courtyard as a storm clears, his white-and-vermillion robes damp and heavy, both hands holding a hichiriki to his lips, centered in the lower third of the frame, back and side profile only. Puddles on the stone courtyard hold faint reflections of the parting clouds. Beside him on a low stone step, a **raiju** — a calm, grounded thunder-beast resembling a wolf or badger with a faint static-like sheen in its fur — sits still and composed, never leaping, never mid-motion. Behind and above him, a colossal standing figure of Raijin takes shape out of the retreating storm clouds — dense, volumetric, deep indigo-violet light with faint electric white-blue patches drifting slowly within, a great ring of taiko drums encircling his back and shoulders on a cord (his classical attribute, carried purely as a still visual prop — see the loop note in §4; the music itself never implies these are being struck). His expression is calm, grounded, immovable — the stillness of thunder that rolls low and even rather than crashing.
- **🔒 Lessons carried forward from HACHIMAN/BENZAITEN/KANNON/FUJIN (apply from the start):**

  - The spirit's body must be DENSE and VOLUMETRIC with uneven internal glow (brighter/dimmer drifting patches) — NOT a flat uniform statue-like surface, NOT a thin outline/wireframe, NOT a carved/cast/statue look of any kind.

  - Hair: long, straight hair, explicitly NOT curly, NOT afro-textured, NOT braided, NO bald patches.

  - The raiju stays still/resting, never leaping or mid-motion, matching the doves/snake/cranes/hawk precedent.
  - **🔒 NO literal lightning bolts crackling across the frame** — a static image of an active lightning strike looks either frozen-wrong or violent in an 8-second loop (same family of problem as the airborne-particles rule). See §6a.

## §1 — Lyria prompts (15 copy-paste variants — hichiriki, explicitly Japanese)

> 🔒 **Anti-genericization rule (carried from BENZAITEN/KANNON/FUJIN).** Every variant names the real **hichiriki** by its actual character — a warm, piercing, slightly buzzing double-reed tone — and explicitly rules out oboe, English horn, bagpipe chanter, or a synth reed/pad substitute. Biwa (deep plucked notes with natural buzzing sawari resonance) and koto (sparse pentatonic bent notes) answer rarely as secondary voices, per the channel's standard secondary-instrument pattern.
> **🔒 No taiko, no percussion, no thunder sound described in the music prompt (LOCKED, carried from the channel-wide NO PERCUSSION rule + the 2026-09-16 background-ambience rule).** Raijin's ring of drums is a purely VISUAL attribute (§0/§3) — never write "taiko," "drum," or any percussive instruction here, it will turn Lyria's output into an actual rhythmic beat and ruin the meditative quality (the exact FUDOSHIN lesson in CLAUDE.md). Thunder/rain atmosphere, if wanted, is added afterward as a separate real SFX bed in CapCut (see §2), same technique as FUJIN's wind.
