## §12 — Community Post (day-of; reuse for concept Short)

SHINRIN-YOKU (森林浴) in Japanese Culture: A Concise Overview

SHINRIN-YOKU (森林浴), literally "forest bathing," is the Japanese practice of slowing down and immersing all the senses in a forest atmosphere. Coined in Japan in the 1980s and since studied by researchers, it is not exercise or hiking but a gentle, unhurried presence among the trees — noticing the light through the leaves (木漏れ日), the smell of the earth, the sound of wind and water. Studies link it to lower stress hormones, blood pressure, and heart rate. At its heart it is a very old idea in new words: that the forest can hold us, and that simply being among living things, breathing slowly, is a kind of healing.
