SHOSHIN (初心) in Japanese Culture: A Concise Overview

初心 (shoshin) means "beginner's mind" — the openness and freshness a beginner brings, unclouded by the assumptions of expertise. It runs from the Noh master Zeami's warning 初心忘るべからず ("never forget the beginner's mind") to the Zen teacher Shunryu Suzuki, whose Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind carried the idea worldwide: "In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities, in the expert's mind there are few." To practice shoshin is to meet each moment as if for the first time. Here a lone monk sits on a temple veranda as the dawn sun rises through the valley mist — a new day, met fresh.
