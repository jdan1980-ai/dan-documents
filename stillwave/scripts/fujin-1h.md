# FUJIN — 風神 | Japanese Zen Music for Letting Go, Release & Inner Peace

## Meta

- **Series:** Kanji-Concept — **KAMI (神) "Gods of Japan" sub-series** — sixth entry (after TSUKUYOMI, AMATERASU, HACHIMAN, BENZAITEN, KANNON).
- **Slug:** fujin-1h
- **Concept:** 風神 (Fūjin), the Shinto/Buddhist god of wind — one of Japan's oldest deities, present at the very beginning of creation in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki. Classical art (most famously Tawaraya Sōtatsu's Fūjin-Raijin folding screen) depicts him as a fierce oni-like figure carrying a great sack of winds (fūtai) slung across his shoulders, releasing gusts by opening its mouth. This series keeps his core identity — the colossal figure and the great wind-bag — but renders him as living light/mist rather than the classical oni look (no horns, no fangs, no monster skin; see §0 lessons). His theme here is **letting go / release** — wind as the force that scatters what no longer needs to be held, clears stale air, carries seeds and thought alike onward. Sacred creature: the **hawk (taka)**, a bird long associated with wind, sky and the mountain passes where Fujin's gusts are said to gather.
- **Playlist (add to in Studio):** Japanese Zen Music
- **Length target:** 1H (continues the HACHIMAN/BENZAITEN/KANNON Lyria-pilot format).
- **Music source:** **Google Lyria**, same anti-genericization approach as BENZAITEN/KANNON. Fujin's signature sound is the **shakuhachi** — the flute literally shaped and played like captured breath/wind — leading every variant, with **wind itself** (through bamboo, pine, or open grassland) as the constant textural bed, deliberately NOT a bell-based signature (bonshō = HACHIMAN, suzu/furin = KANNON already claimed those).
- **Status:** 🟡 IN PROGRESS — package drafted, no assets generated yet. **Next: generate hero (§3), then the 10 Lyria tracks (§1).**

## §0 — Positioning & pre-production

- **🔒 KAMI series invariants (carried over):** each god manifests as a colossal figure formed of LIVING LIGHT/MIST — never a statue, never a carved idol, never a painted mural. A lone human figure anchors scale. Each god gets its own signature light colour — **FUJIN = cool storm-grey with a pale jade-green undertone**, explicitly distinct from Tsukuyomi's pale silver-blue, Amaterasu's warm golden-amber, Hachiman's warm white-gold, Benzaiten's cool aquamarine-teal, and Kannon's soft rose-pink/pearl-white.
- **🔒 Break from the classical oni look — MANDATORY correction for this god specifically.** Fujin is traditionally painted as a wild, fanged, horned, green-or-red-skinned demon figure (the Sōtatsu screen). That reads as a monster, not a living-light deity, and would break the series' entire visual language. Explicitly exclude: NO horns, NO fangs, NO clawed hands, NO monstrous or oni-like face, NO green or red demon skin, NO grimacing or threatening expression. Keep only what belongs to the series: a colossal figure of dense, volumetric, uneven-glowing light in the signature colour, calm-but-powerful rather than menacing — closer in spirit to HACHIMAN's protector-warmth than to a wrathful spirit.
- **Departure from the samurai convention (continues BENZAITEN/KANNON's precedent):** Fujin's human anchor is a **lone komusō** — a wandering Zen mendicant monk of the historical Fuke sect, instantly recognizable by his woven straw basket hat (tengai) that fully covers his face (a natural, authentic way to keep the figure anonymous without relying on "back to camera," and it doubles as the very instrument-player this video's sound is built around). He carries no weapon — only his shakuhachi, held to his lips or resting in both hands.
- **Visual hook:** a lone komusō monk stands on a windswept mountain ridge at dusk, his tengai hat hiding his face entirely, his plain grey-brown robes and sash caught mid-motion by a strong steady wind, both hands raised with his shakuhachi at his lips as if just having played a note. Below and around him, a sea of tall pampas grass (susuki) bends and streams in the same wind toward the horizon. Behind and above him, a colossal standing figure of Fujin takes shape out of the moving air itself — dense, volumetric, storm-grey and pale jade-green light, the great wind-bag (fūtai) slung diagonally across his back and shoulders, both hands gripping its cords, one open end held slightly forward from which a continuous, glowing stream of wind/mist pours outward and dissolves into the real wind moving the grass below. A single hawk sits calm and still on a weathered rock outcrop near the monk, wings folded, watching the horizon, never in flight. Small wooden wind-chimes or a torii gate may sit distant and still in the scene, undisturbed by the very wind that moves the grass around them (the god's wind is felt, not literalized as chaos in the whole frame).
- **🔒 Lessons carried forward from HACHIMAN/BENZAITEN/KANNON (apply from the start):**
  - Fully photoreal, restrained, cinematic — NOT video-game key-art, NOT stylized/cartoonish/illustrated.
  - The spirit's body must be DENSE and VOLUMETRIC with uneven internal glow (brighter/dimmer drifting patches) — NOT a flat uniform statue-like surface, NOT a thin outline/wireframe, NOT a carved/cast/statue look of any kind.
  - Explicit color anchor stated positively AND negatively (cool storm-grey with a pale jade-green undertone, explicitly NOT teal, NOT aquamarine, NOT gold, NOT rose-pink, NOT silver-blue).
  - Hair (where visible on the spirit): long, straight, streaming hair caught in the same wind, explicitly NOT curly, NOT afro-textured, NOT braided, NO bald patches.
  - No aggressive pose — despite being a wind/storm deity, keep the gesture powerful-but-calm (releasing, not attacking); this is a "letting go" video, so the mood is release and relief, never violence or threat.
  - The hawk stays still/resting, never mid-flight, matching the doves/snake/cranes precedent.
  - **🔒 NO AIRBORNE PARTICLES rule still applies even though this is a WIND video** — the wind itself is shown through the grass bending, robes/sash caught mid-motion, and the glowing stream from the wind-bag, never through loose leaves/petals/debris flying through the air (those freeze badly in the loop, same lesson as MAKOTO's maple leaves). The swaying susuki grass is the approved substitute per the channel's locked alternative list.
  - Compose the frame naturally — no requirement to darken either corner (corner-darkening retired channel-wide 2026-09-15); the text/logo added in post already carries its own scrim for contrast.
- **Wisdom phrase is NOT 風神 itself** (overlay ≠ title concept, series rule). Chosen: **諸行無常** (shogyō mujō — "all things are impermanent"), the opening line of the Heike Monogatari and one of Buddhism's most fundamental truths — everything that can be scattered by wind (dust, cloud, thought, sorrow) already carries this nature within it, without naming Fujin directly. See §6a.
- **🇺🇦** Шестая запись серии KAMI. Фудзин (風神) — синтоистско-буддийский бог ветра, одно из древнейших божеств Японии, в классическом искусстве (ширма Тавараи Сотацу) изображён как свирепый демон-óни с мешком ветров за плечами. Серия сохраняет мешок ветров как ключевой атрибут, но убирает демонический облик (рога, клыки, монстрская кожа) — вместо этого живой свет в духе всей серии. Тема — «отпускание/освобождение»: ветер как сила, разносящая то, что больше не нужно держать. Спутник — ястреб (символ ветра и неба). Человек-якорь — странствующий монах-комусо в плетёной шляпе тэнгай, скрывающей лицо, играющий на сякухати (флейта = «пойманное дыхание/ветер»). Цвет свечения — холодный грозовой серый с бледно-нефритовым оттенком. Мудрость — не сам 風神, а 諸行無常 («всё преходяще»).

FUJIN (風神) in Japanese Culture: A Concise Overview

風神 (Fūjin), the god of wind, is one of Japan's oldest deities, present at the very beginning of creation in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki. Classical art — most famously Tawaraya Sōtatsu's Fūjin-Raijin folding screen — depicts him carrying a great sack of winds across his shoulders, releasing gusts by opening its mouth. Wind in Japanese thought is closely tied to 諸行無常 (shogyō mujō), the Buddhist truth that all things are impermanent — the same force that scatters fallen leaves also carries away what no longer needs to be held. His companion here is the hawk, long associated with wind and the high mountain passes. Here a lone komusō monk plays the shakuhachi — an instrument literally shaped around captured breath — as the wind god himself takes form from the moving air around him.
