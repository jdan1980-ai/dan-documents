GAMAN (我慢) in Japanese Culture: A Concise Overview

GAMAN (我慢) is the Japanese practice of enduring difficulty with patience, composure, and quiet dignity. Rooted in Zen Buddhist and Confucian thought, the word carries the sense of bearing what cannot be changed — not passively, but as a conscious act of inner strength. During the Edo period, the samurai codes of conduct elevated gaman as a supreme virtue: the warrior who does not react is more powerful than the one who does. In the postwar era, gaman became the cultural spine of Japan's collective rebuilding — the quiet resolve of a people outlasting the unendurable. As the proverb goes: 石の上にも三年 — "Sit on a stone for three years, and even the stone grows warm."
