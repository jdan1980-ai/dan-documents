## §0 — Positioning & pre-production

- **🔒 KAMI series invariants (carried over from `hachiman-2h.md`):** each god manifests as a colossal figure formed of LIVING LIGHT/MIST — never a statue, never a carved idol, never a painted mural. A lone human figure anchors scale — **for Tsukuyomi specifically, a seated monk in quiet zazen meditation, not a standing samurai** (see note below on why the pose changes per-god). Each god gets its own **signature light colour** — **TSUKUYOMI = pale silver-blue moonlight**.
- **🔒 NEW pattern this video — the god's POSE matches their domain, not just their colour (locked 2026-09-01):** HACHIMAN stood, armored, bow raised — an active guardian. TSUKUYOMI is a quiet, withdrawn, contemplative deity, so he is seated in meditation (based on the classical iconographic print the user supplied as reference — cross-legged, calm, one hand holding a white lotus/flower, the other holding a gohei — a Shinto paper-streamer wand — moon rabbits resting at his base). Future gods should follow the same logic: let the deity's actual mythological temperament dictate standing/seated/active/still, rather than reusing one fixed pose for every video.
- **TSUKUYOMI — visual hook:** a lone monk sits in quiet zazen on a rock at the edge of a still lake or sea at night, facing the water where the full moon is reflected. Above and beyond the water, a colossal seated figure of Tsukuyomi takes shape out of the moonlight and night mist itself — robed, calm-faced, haloed in pale silver-blue radiance, one hand holding a white lotus flower, the other holding a gohei wand, cross-legged in meditation. Two pale, faintly luminous moon rabbits rest at the base of the manifestation, one nestled on either side, per the *tsuki no usagi* folklore. The god's visible torso, face, and the rabbits are "made of" the moonlight; the lower folds of his robe dissolve into drifting night mist.
- **🔒 No falling/floating petals, no random airborne particles — do not mention them even to forbid them** (locked rule). No doves/birds needed here (that was Hachiman's signature); Tsukuyomi's signature companions are the two still, resting moon rabbits — deliberately NOT animated in flight, just present and calm.
- **🔒 Any light source that should stay constant (the moon, the manifestation's glow) must be described as ALREADY THERE from the first frame** — never fading in mid-loop (SUIRYU lesson).
- **No in-image text anywhere** — the classical print reference has a kanji name-cartouche baked into it; that is dropped entirely from our generated art (channel-wide no-AI-text rule) and handled only in post via the compose script.
- **Wisdom phrase is NOT 月読 itself** (overlay ≠ title concept). Chosen: **明月清風** (meigetsu seifū — "a bright moon, a clear wind"), a classical Zen phrase describing a mind as pure and unclouded as a moonlit night. See §6a.
- **🇺🇦** Флагманом серии KAMI открываем **TSUKUYOMI**, а не Hachiman (тот остаётся готовым черновиком, `hachiman-2h.md`, — выпустим позже). Новое правило серии: поза бога отражает его характер, а не одна и та же поза для всех — Хатиман стоял с луком, Цукуёми сидит в медитации (по найденной иконографии): гохэй в одной руке, белый цветок в другой, лунные зайцы у ног (фольклор о зайце, толкущем моти на луне). Монах в дзадзэн вместо самурая — тоже осознанный выбор под тихий, отстранённый характер лунного бога. Мудрость на оверлее — 明月清風 (мэйгэцу сэйфу:, «ясная луна, чистый ветер»), не сам 月読.

TSUKUYOMI (月読) in Japanese Culture: A Concise Overview

月読 (Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto), the god of the moon, was born from the right eye of the primordial god Izanagi, brother to Amaterasu (the sun) and Susanoo (storms and sea). Where his siblings are vivid and active in Japanese myth, Tsukuyomi is famously quiet and withdrawn — so much so that after one disagreement with the goddess Uke Mochi, he and Amaterasu are said to have separated forever, which the old stories give as the reason day and night never meet. His name itself means something like "moon-counter," tied to the moon's role in measuring the months. He shares the sky with the beloved *tsuki no usagi* — the moon rabbit, said to be visible pounding rice cakes in the moon's own markings.
