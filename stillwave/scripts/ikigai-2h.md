IKIGAI (生き甲斐) in Japanese Culture: A Concise Overview

IKIGAI (生き甲斐) combines iki ("to live") and gai ("worth") — the thing that makes life feel worth living. Unlike the Western search for one grand purpose, Japanese tradition finds ikigai in the small and daily: the craft perfected over decades, the garden tended each morning, the person you pour tea for. The psychiatrist Mieko Kamiya, whose 1966 work defined the concept, described ikigai as what allows a person to look forward to tomorrow — even in hardship. In Okinawa, home to some of the world's longest-lived people, elders rarely retire from their ikigai at all; as the saying goes, 好きこそ物の上手なれ — "What you love, you master."
